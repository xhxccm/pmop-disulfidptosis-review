**Figure 1. Molecular mechanism of disulfidptosis.**
Under glucose-sufficient conditions (left), glucose-derived NADPH via the pentose phosphate pathway sustains cystine reduction by SLC7A11, maintaining thiol homeostasis and cell survival. Under glucose starvation (right), NADPH production is blocked while SLC7A11 continues to import cystine, leading to NADPH depletion, disulfide stress, and aberrant disulfide bond formation within actin cytoskeletal proteins (e.g., ACTB, FLNA, RAC1, NCKAP1). The resulting F-actin collapse and cell contraction define disulfidptosis, which can be rescued by disulfide-reducing agents but not by ROS scavengers or apoptosis/ferroptosis inhibitors. Original schematic drawn by the authors based on the mechanism described in Liu et al., *Nat Cell Biol* 2023 [ref 6]; no material was reproduced from the original publication, and no permission is required.

**Figure 2. A standard multi-omics biomarker discovery pipeline and its associated pitfalls.**
Seven sequential steps (blue boxes, left) are paired with the methodological pitfall most likely to compromise each step (red boxes, right): (1) data acquisition — tissue/cell-type mismatch and batch effects; (2) preprocessing — inappropriate scaling; (3) differential expression — threshold choice (FDR vs nominal P) determines the gene list; (4) enrichment — gene-set version and size sensitivity; (5) feature selection — data leakage when selection is performed outside cross-validation; (6) model building — AUC near 1.0 in small cohorts as a red flag; (7) external validation — mandatory yet frequently omitted, with calibration and decision-curve analysis required. The bottom bar lists the reporting frameworks recommended for transparent disclosure (TRIPOD+AI, PROBAST+AI, EQUATOR Network). This schematic serves as the organizational framework for Sections 3–4 of this review.

**Figure 3. Worked example — differential expression and threshold sensitivity.**
(A) Volcano plot of the case dataset (GSE56815; 20 PMOP vs 20 control peripheral blood mononuclear cells) showing 333 FDR-significant differentially expressed genes. (B) Venn diagram of disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) intersected with DEGs under two thresholds: FDR < 0.05 (2 genes) versus nominal P < 0.05 (9 genes). The contrast illustrates how a seemingly technical threshold choice directly reshapes the downstream biological narrative. Representative case analysis redrawn from the authors' re-analysis of public GEO data; original figure `figures/Fig2A_volcano.png`, `figures/Fig2C_DRG_DEG_venn.png`. This panel is presented solely as an illustrative worked example, not as a novel finding.

**Figure 4. Worked example — three machine-learning feature-selection strategies and their intersection.**
(A) LASSO coefficient profiles with cross-validated selection; (B) SVM-RFE recursive feature elimination ranking; (C) random-forest feature importance (Gini and permutation); (D) three-algorithm intersection yielding an 18-gene signature. While intersecting multiple algorithms can improve robustness, it does not constitute independent validation — selections may simply converge on low-variance genes. Note that the SVM-RFE step achieved a cross-validated AUC of 1.0, an indicator of likely overfitting in this small cohort. Case analysis redrawn from `figures/Fig3A–3D`; see Section 3 for methodological discussion.

**Figure 5. Worked example — an apparently perfect internal model that fails external validation.**
(A) Forest-style comparison of AUC values: 1.000 in the training cohort (GSE56815, PBMC), 0.340 in external cohort GSE7429 (B cells), and 0.572 in GSE56814 (cross-platform). The chance line (AUC = 0.5) is shown for reference; the GSE7429 AUC below 0.5 indicates direction reversal rather than mere performance decay. The inset annotation summarizes the regularization sensitivity analysis (C = 1, 0.1, 0.01): training AUC remains 1.00 while external AUC stays in the 0.35–0.50 range, indicating that hyperparameter tuning cannot rescue generalization. (B) Conceptual explanation: the training and validation cohorts differ in cell lineage (PBMC vs B cells) and platform, so the same genes operate in a different cellular context, producing a cohort-internal signature rather than a transportable diagnostic marker. Data from `diagnostic_results/external_validation.csv` and `regularization_comparison.csv`. R and Python implementations gave identical AUC values, confirming the result is not implementation-specific.

**Figure 6. Evidence hierarchy of computational "validation" approaches.**
Five tiers ordered by the strength of inference they support. Tiers 1–3 (public databases and literature; public single-cell data; perturbation-signature resources such as DepMap, LINCS/CMap and Perturb-seq) generate and prioritize hypotheses but cannot establish causality or phenotype. Tiers 4–5 (cell-based knockdown/overexpression with rescue; animal models with tissue-level endpoints) are required to confirm mechanism and efficacy. The filled circles at right indicate increasing evidence strength from bottom to top. This hierarchy underpins the discussion in Sections 5–7.

---

**Figure 7. Coverage of wet-lab experiments by computational and literature-based alternatives.**
A five-level ladder showing which experimental readouts can be substituted by public databases, published literature, single-cell sequencing, or virtual knockout, and which cannot. Molecular/expression readouts and cell-type localization can be fully replaced by public resources; perturbation transcriptomics is partially replaceable; cell functional phenotypes require a minimal wet-lab experiment; and in vivo efficacy (e.g., OVX + Micro-CT) is not replaceable. Adapted from the evidence-coverage discussion in Section 7.

---

**Figure 8. A practical checklist for transparent reporting of ML-based biomarker studies.**
Three categories adapted from TRIPOD+AI (27 items) and PROBAST+AI: (i) data and design — source, sample size, eligibility, pre-specified external cohort, ethics and availability; (ii) methods and cross-validation — feature selection inside training folds (nested CV), preprocessing, hyperparameters, repeated CV, software versions; (iii) performance and reporting — discrimination with confidence intervals, calibration, decision-curve analysis, subgroup fairness, and honest external validation even when AUC < 0.5. Full checklists are available at tripod-statement.org and probast.org. The bottom line emphasizes that a near-perfect AUC in a small cohort warrants scrutiny rather than celebration.
